Tools for a search that now answers back.

People don't only type a search and click a link anymore. They ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI answers, and they get a reply before they ever see a website. Many brand websites were built for the old way, and the drop shows up in traffic long before anyone can explain it.

So I'm building a tool for small Shopify stores. It checks whether AI assistants understand their products, shows what they get wrong, and helps the owner fix it in plain words. Some people call this AEO, answer engine optimisation.

Product · Shopify stores · Canada and US first

Clearshelf

Planning and designing in Figma

Most small store owners aren't technical. The tools that exist either list problems without fixing them, or bury the answer in jargon. Clearshelf checks the store, tests it with real AI questions, and helps write the fixes.

  • Store check. Does each product description explain who it's for, what's in it and how to use it? Does the name say what the product is? Are common questions answered? Results in plain words, with real numbers and steps to fix each issue.
  • "Ask AI" test. Asks the questions real shoppers ask, then shows whether the brand was named, who was named instead, and which pages AI used as sources.
  • Description writer. A few short questions about the product, then a clear description, name and FAQ, written only from facts the owner confirms. It gently flags risky claims like "cures".
  • FAQ builder. My collapsible FAQ generator becomes part of it: tidy FAQ tabs for Shopify themes, with the label AI tools read.

The promise is honest: make products easy for AI to understand and find. Not "AI will recommend you", because that also depends on reviews and what other websites say.

Why I'm doing this

I'd rather build the fix than explain the drop.

For four years I sat with founders looking at numbers that had moved without warning. Search is moving faster than most teams can keep up with, and smaller brands feel it first. Building these tools keeps me close to how the change actually works, and it gives my clients something useful today.

I'll share what I learn here as I go.
